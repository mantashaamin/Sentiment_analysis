PHASE 1: DATASET EXPLORATION
================================================================

1. OBJECTIVE

The objective of this phase was to understand the selected
Google Play Store reviews dataset before performing data
cleaning, NLP preprocessing and machine learning.

We explored the dataset structure, columns, missing values,
duplicate reviews, star ratings, application distribution,
sentiment distribution and review lengths.

The purpose was to understand the quality and characteristics
of our data before making further implementation decisions.


2. DATASET INFORMATION

Dataset: Google Play Store App Reviews

Dataset file:
dataset/raw/google_play_reviews.csv

Total records: 16,092
Total columns: 12
Total applications: 15
Unique review texts: 10,663

The dataset contains reviews from multiple productivity and
personal-organisation applications, including task management,
habit tracking, focus, planning and calendar applications.

Important columns:

- content: Actual review text.
- score: Star rating given by the user.
- appId: Application identifier.

Other columns include reviewId, userName, userImage,
thumbsUpCount, reviewCreatedVersion, at, replyContent,
repliedAt and sortOrder.

6. DUPLICATE ANALYSIS

Observed results:

Total records:                 16,092
Completely duplicated rows:         0
Repeated review text occurrences: 5,429
Unique review texts:           10,663

Further investigation:

We grouped reviews by their exact text and checked whether
the same text had different star ratings.

Result:

64 unique review texts were found with conflicting ratings.

Examples:

Review: Amazing app
Ratings: 3, 4, 5

Review: Awesome
Ratings: 4, 5

Review: Best
Ratings: 1, 3

Review: Cool
Ratings: 4, 5

Why this matters:

Our sentiment labels are generated from star ratings.
Therefore, identical review texts with different ratings
can receive different sentiment labels.

Decision:

We will not blindly remove all repeated reviews.

We need to investigate conflicting review groups before
finalising duplicate handling.

We must also prevent identical review texts from appearing
in both training and testing data, wherever practical,
to avoid data leakage.

8. SENTIMENT LABEL CREATION

We proposed converting numerical star ratings into
three sentiment categories.

Mapping:

1-2 Stars = Negative
3 Stars   = Neutral
4-5 Stars = Positive

Derived sentiment distribution:

Sentiment       Reviews       Percentage

Negative         5,188          32.24%
Neutral          5,150          32.00%
Positive         5,754          35.76%

Total           16,092         100%

Observation:

The three sentiment classes are reasonably balanced.

This is useful because our classification model will not
face a severe overall class imbalance.

Important limitation:

These sentiment labels are derived from star ratings,
not independently annotated by human reviewers.

A three-star review may contain both positive and negative
opinions. Therefore, our model learns to predict
rating-derived sentiment labels.

We must clearly mention this limitation in our final report.

12. RESEARCH PAPER CONNECTION

PAPER 1:

Research idea used:

The paper provides an overview of sentiment analysis,
text classification, feature representation and challenges
in opinion mining.

Our implementation:

We use its general sentiment-analysis foundations to guide
our dataset understanding and subsequent NLP methodology.

PHASE 2: DATA CLEANING
================================================================

1. OBJECTIVE

The objective of this phase was to clean the original dataset,
remove ambiguous records, handle duplicate combinations,
retain relevant columns and prepare a structured dataset
for NLP preprocessing and machine learning.

The original dataset was preserved without modification.


2. TOOLS AND LIBRARIES USED

Python 3:
Used for implementing the data-cleaning pipeline.

Pandas:
Used for handling missing values, grouping reviews,
identifying conflicting labels, removing duplicates,
creating sentiment labels and saving the cleaned dataset.

Pathlib:
Used for managing project directories and file paths.


3. IMPLEMENTATION

Created and implemented:

src/data_cleaning.py

The script performs the following operations:

1. Loads the original CSV dataset.
2. Selects the required columns:
   - content
   - score
   - appId
3. Handles missing and empty review text.
4. Identifies review texts having conflicting ratings.
5. Saves conflicting records separately.
6. Excludes conflicting review groups.
7. Removes repeated combinations of content, score and appId.
8. Creates three sentiment labels from star ratings.
9. Saves the cleaned dataset.
10. Verifies the final dataset.


4. CONFLICTING REVIEW HANDLING

From Phase 1, we identified:

Conflicting review groups: 64
Total records in those groups: 429

Examples included:

- Not good
- Not free
- Amazing app
- Awesome
- Good
- Nice

The same review text appeared with different star ratings,
which could produce contradictory sentiment labels.

Decision:

All records belonging to the 64 conflicting review groups
were excluded from the primary cleaned dataset.

The original conflicting records were saved separately
for reference and further analysis.

Reason:

Our model uses review text as input. Identical text with
different sentiment labels can introduce ambiguity during
supervised learning.

Limitation:

Some repeated short reviews may represent genuine user
opinions. Excluding these records is a methodological
choice to reduce label ambiguity, not proof that those
reviews were incorrect.


5. DUPLICATE REMOVAL

After excluding conflicting review groups:

Records remaining: 15,663

Repeated combinations removed: 5,024

The following columns were used to identify repeated
combinations:

- content
- score
- appId

Pandas drop_duplicates() was used to retain one occurrence
of each identical combination.

Important:

This operation removes repeated combinations, not
necessarily accidental duplicate submissions. Different
users may independently submit identical short reviews.

We will also account for identical review texts when
designing our training and testing strategy.


6. MISSING AND EMPTY VALUE HANDLING

The script checked the required columns:

- content
- score
- appId

Missing or empty records removed: 0

All three required columns were complete in the
original dataset.

Optional metadata columns were excluded because they
were not required for the initial sentiment classifier.
