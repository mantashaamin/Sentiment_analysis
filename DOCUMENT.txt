# PHASE 3: NLP TEXT PREPROCESSING

## 1. Objective

The objective of this phase is to prepare the cleaned Google Play Store reviews for machine learning by removing unnecessary textual elements while preserving important sentiment-related information.

Raw user reviews contain uppercase letters, punctuation, URLs, HTML tags, contractions, common words and irregular whitespace. These elements need to be handled before converting text into numerical features.

The preprocessing process is applied to the review text stored in the `content` column.

## 2. Input Dataset

**Input file:**

`dataset/processed/cleaned_reviews.csv`

**Initial number of records:** 10,639

The dataset contains the following columns:

- `content`: Original user review.
- `score`: Star rating given by the user.
- `appId`: Unique application identifier.
- `sentiment`: Sentiment label derived from the star rating.

## 3. Preprocessing Techniques Applied

The following NLP preprocessing operations were implemented.

### A. Lowercasing

All review text was converted into lowercase to maintain consistency and avoid treating uppercase and lowercase versions of the same word as different terms.

Example:

Original: `GREAT Application`

Processed: `great application`

### B. Contraction Expansion

Common English contractions were expanded before punctuation removal to preserve their original meaning.

Examples:

- don't → do not
- doesn't → does not
- isn't → is not
- can't → cannot
- won't → will not
- shouldn't → should not

This is particularly important because contractions frequently express negation.

### C. URL Removal

Website links and URLs were removed because they generally do not contribute directly to the sentiment expressed in a review.

Example:

Original: `Visit www.example.com for details`

Processed: `visit for details`

### D. HTML Tag Removal

HTML tags were removed to retain only readable textual content.

Example:

Original: `<b>Good application</b>`

Processed: `good application`

### E. Punctuation and Special Character Removal

Punctuation marks, numbers and unnecessary special characters were removed using regular expressions.

Only English alphabetic characters and whitespace were retained.

Example:

Original: `Amazing app!!! 10/10`

Processed: `amazing app`

### F. Whitespace Normalization

Multiple spaces, tabs and unnecessary whitespace were replaced with a single space.

This ensures consistent formatting throughout the dataset.

### G. Stopword Removal

Common English stopwords were removed using the English stopword corpus provided by the NLTK library.

Stopwords are frequently occurring words that may contribute less useful information to basic text classification.

Examples include:

- the
- is
- are
- an
- a

However, not every common word was removed. Important sentiment-related words were preserved.

### H. Preservation of Important Sentiment Words

Certain words were deliberately excluded from stopword removal because they can change or strengthen the meaning of a review.

**1. Negation words**

Examples: no, not, never, neither, none.

**2. Contrast words**

Examples: but, however, although, though, yet.

**3. Intensity and emphasis words**

Examples: very, too, really, extremely, quite, highly.

**4. Other meaningful words**

Examples: only, just, barely, hardly, still, without.

Preserving these words helps retain important sentiment information.

For example:

- `not good` should not become `good`.
- `good but expensive` should retain the contrast expressed by `but`.
- `very useful` should retain the intensity expressed by `very`.

### I. Removal of Empty Processed Reviews

Some reviews contained only punctuation, numbers, special characters or stopwords.

After preprocessing, these reviews became empty and were removed because they did not contain useful textual information for model training.

## 4. Implementation Details

The preprocessing logic was implemented in:

`src/preprocessing.py`

|---|---|

The function was applied to the `content` column using Pandas.

A new column named `cleaned_content` was created to store the processed reviews.

The original `content` column was retained so that the original and processed versions could be compared during later experiments.

## 8. Important Methodological Decisions

The following decisions were made during preprocessing:

1. Negation words were preserved because removing them could reverse the meaning of a review.
2. Contrast words were preserved because they help identify changes in opinion within a sentence.
3. Intensity words were preserved because they indicate the strength of an opinion.
4. Stemming and lemmatization were not applied in this phase to avoid unnecessary modification of the original vocabulary.
5. The original review text was retained alongside processed text for future experimentation.
6. Reviews that became empty after preprocessing were removed.
7. The final processed dataset was verified for missing values.

## 9. Limitations

- Sentiment labels are derived from star ratings and may not always match the actual emotion expressed in the review.
- Stopword removal may discard some contextual information.
- Sarcasm, spelling mistakes and informal expressions may still affect sentiment classification.
- The preprocessing approach is primarily designed for English-language reviews.
- Some meaningful contextual information may be lost when punctuation and numbers are removed.

Library:

4. TF-IDF

Library:
Scikit-learn TfidfVectorizer

Configuration:
- max_features = 5000
- ngram_range = (1, 2)
- min_df = 2

Description:

TF-IDF stands for Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency.

It assigns weights to words based on their frequency within
a document and how common they are across the training
document collection.

Common words generally receive lower IDF weights, while
more distinctive words can receive higher weights.

The vocabulary and IDF values were learned only from
training data. Testing data was transformed using the
same fitted vectorizer.

Results:
- Vocabulary size: 5,000
- Training matrix: 8,461 x 5,000
- Testing matrix: 2,114 x 5,000

Saved files:
- X_train_tfidf.npz
- X_test_tfidf.npz
- tfidf_vectorizer.pkl

6. REVIEW-LEVEL WORD2VEC REPRESENTATION

Word2Vec produces a vector for each individual word.
However, machine learning classifiers require one
fixed-size vector for each review.

Therefore, the average of all available word vectors
in a review was calculated.

Each review is represented by a 100-dimensional vector.

If a review contains no words present in the trained
Word2Vec vocabulary, a zero vector is returned.

This method is known as average word embedding.

- CBOW and Skip-gram produced 100-dimensional review
  representations.
- Both Word2Vec models were saved and reloaded.
- Both Word2Vec models contained 4,953 vocabulary words.
- No identical original review groups overlapped between
  training and testing datasets.

The saved feature matrices and models were successfully
loaded during verification.


10. LIMITATIONS

- BoW considers word frequency but does not understand
  semantic meaning.
- TF-IDF assigns importance weights but does not understand
  sentence context.
- Word2Vec learns word relationships but does not directly
  represent complete sentence meaning.
- Averaging Word2Vec vectors loses word order and some
  contextual information.
- The group-aware split prevents identical review text
  leakage, but it does not guarantee generalization to
  completely unseen applications.

These limitations will be considered during model
evaluation and result interpretation.


11. CONCLUSION

In this phase, preprocessed Google Play reviews were
converted into numerical feature representations using
Bag-of-Words, TF-IDF, CBOW and Skip-gram.

All feature matrices and trained vectorizers or embedding
models were saved successfully.

The generated features are ready to be used in the next
phase for training and evaluating machine learning
classification algorithms.
